Mapping the Recoverability Boundary: Cross-Level Information Sharing and Intermittent Modeling for Sparse IP-Level Network Telemetry

Independent research

Investigating why forecasting accuracy collapses at the fine-grained IP level (R² ≈ -1.39) on the CESNET-TimeSeries24 dataset, a large-scale network telemetry dataset spanning 275,000+ network entities.

  • Decomposing the forecasting failure into its component causes — intermittency, magnitude error, and observation gaps — rather than treating it as one undifferentiated problem
  • Developing cross-level information sharing methods that exploit the dataset's IP → subnet → institution hierarchy, combining foundation model in-context learning with forecast reconciliation to stabilize sparse IP-level predictions using more stable aggregate signals
  • Working toward an empirical "recoverability boundary": a precise characterization of where sparse network telemetry can and cannot be reliably forecast, aimed at operational guidance usable at ISP scale

High-Performance Fano Resonant All-Dielectric Metasurface for Near-Infrared Refractive Index Sensing

Undergraduate thesis · Supervisor: Md. Aslam Mollah

  • Designed and optimized a novel asymmetric silicon dimer metasurface sensor through systematic, simulation-driven parameter sweeps, achieving sensing performance exceeding initial design targets (723.14 nm/RIU bulk sensitivity, 0.479 nm FWHM linewidth, quality factor of 2,583)
  • Identified electric dipole–magnetic quadrupole (ED-MQ) interference as the primary mechanism driving sharp Fano resonance formation, enabled by a 45 nm coupling overlap between asymmetric discs
  • Developed a reproducible four-phase design methodology (architecture definition → design-space exploration → systematic optimization → performance validation) for iterative refinement of complex electromagnetic systems
